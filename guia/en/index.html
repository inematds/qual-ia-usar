Which AI to useGEMINI - GPT - CLAUDE

The cheapest AI on the price list may cost you more in the end.

You choose an AI based on its price list. It makes a mistake, you ask again, and every attempt costs you: money if you pay as you go, or your quota if you have a subscription. Here you can compare Google, Anthropic, and OpenAI AIs by their cost per successful task: what you spend before you get the task done.

Who wins in each tier Test with your task
In one sentence

This project compares ten AI models from three companies: Google's Gemini 4 (Neon, Argon, and Hydrogen), Anthropic's Claude (Fable, Opus, Sonnet, and Haiku), and the OpenAI models used by Codex (Astra, Sol, and Luna). It brings together official price lists and the Agent Arena leaderboard, a public leaderboard that measures AI agents (AIs that carry out a task on their own, step by step) doing real work, checked on Nov 19, 2026. It's for people who pay for AI and want to choose without wasting money. You don't need anything to read it. To test with your own task, all you need are the apps you already use (ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini), or the testing kit, which runs the same task on every AI and tells you which one got more right and spent less.

What you gain

Choose by the price list or by successful task

AIs charge by token, the pieces of text they read and write. You open the price list, see that Gemini 4 Hydrogen charges less per token than Argon, and choose it.

In practice

"How Nei chooses"

The numbers show what it costs. Using the models shows what's worth it. These are Nei's everyday choices.

🪶 Luna and Haiku: affordable for simple, lengthy work

GPT-6 Luna and Claude Haiku 5.5 cost pennies and got the simple task in our test right. They're good options for simple, lengthy work: many repeated steps, high volume, tasks that don't require deep reasoning.

⭐ Terra and Sonnet: Nei's favorites

In Nei's tests, his favorites are GPT-6.1 Terra and Claude Sonnet 5.5: they cost more than Luna and Haiku, but deliver good results more consistently. At OpenAI, the order from cheapest to most expensive is Luna, Terra, Sol, and Astra. Terra is not included in the tables above.

🔍 Neon as the critic

Nei currently has Claude and Codex review each other's work: models from different companies make different mistakes. Gemini 4 Neon can provide that second look, checking and critiquing the work if you only have Claude or only have Codex. See how to connect Gemini Pro.

Gemini Pro in Claude and Codex

Use your Gemini subscription inside Claude Code or Codex

You pay for Google AI Pro and want Gemini to work alongside Claude or Codex, for example as an AI that checks and critiques? You can, but the process changed in 2026.

⚠️ What changed: Gemini CLI has been retired

Gemini CLI was the way to use a Gemini subscription in the terminal. On June 18, 2026, Google stopped accepting Pro or Ultra subscribers' accounts in it. Anyone who tries to sign in today gets the message: "This client is no longer supported for Gemini Code Assist for individuals".

The replacement is Antigravity CLI, Google's Antigravity terminal program. It uses your subscription quota.

🔑 Why an API key won't work

A Gemini subscription doesn't apply to Google AI Studio API keys. The key has its own quota: on the free plan, Hydrogen responds, but Neon and Argon return "quota exceeded." For those models, you need a key linked to a credit card and pay as you go.

  1. We tried the API keys we already had.
    All were on the free plan: Hydrogen ran, while Neon and Argon returned "quota exceeded."
  2. We tried Gemini CLI with the subscription account.
    Login worked, but Google rejected its use and told us to migrate to Antigravity.
  3. We installed Antigravity CLI and signed in with a Google account.
    It shows a link; you sign in with the account that has the Gemini subscription, copy the code that appears, and paste it back. The list included Gemini 4 Neon, Argon, and Hydrogen at several effort levels, as well as Claude Opus and Sonnet.
  4. Gemini started running through the subscription, called from inside Claude Code.
    The testing kit ran all three Gemini models this way, and all three got the receipts task right.

Think of a contractor who charges little for a day's work but rebuilds the wall three times. The day rate was cheap; the wall wasn't. AI works the same way: what matters is how much you paid before the task was done right.

How to get started

Find out which AI costs less for your task

The leaderboard is a good starting point, but the right answer comes from your task. There are two options: do it yourself with the apps you already subscribe to, or have your AI set up the test for you.

✋ Option 1: do it yourself, with your existing subscriptions

You don't need to install anything or create a new account. Don't subscribe to all three? They have free versions with limits that are enough for the test.

  1. Choose a real work task and decide in advance what counts as "done right."
  2. Ask for the same task 3 times in each app, paid or free (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini), always in a new conversation.
  3. Write down how many times out of 3 it was right without your corrections.
  4. In the calculator above, enter the minutes you spend on each attempt (asking, reading, and checking) under "cost per attempt," along with the attempts and successful results you recorded. The lowest result is the one that uses less of your time and quota.

🤖 Option 2: the testing kit runs your task on every AI you enable

Describe the task and what counts as "done right." The kit sends the same task to each AI several times, an evaluator checks each response, and you get a report showing how many were successful, how long it took, and the cost per successful task. Your AI sets up and runs the kit.

1. Open your AI

Claude Code (included with a Claude subscription) or Codex (included with ChatGPT): these are versions of these AIs that work with files on your computer. Never used them? Stick with option 1.

2. Paste the prompt below

It tells the AI to read the kit, format your task for it, and show you the plan without running anything yet.

3. Approve the test

Claude and GPT run through Claude Code and Codex subscriptions. Gemini runs through a Google AI Pro subscription with Antigravity CLI (see how to connect it). Outside subscriptions, you'll need a pay-as-you-go account (the API) with a credit card. You approve which models to include, and the AI returns the report.

Read the project "Which AI to use" at https://github.com/inematds/qual-ia-usar (the README explains the testing kit in kit/testar.py).
I want to know which AI costs less per successful task for one of my tasks: [describe the task and what counts as "done right"].
1. In plain English, tell me which 3 or 4 models are worth testing for this task and why.

The proof

The math changes the answer

  1. Per token, Hydrogen is Google's cheapest.

    How many tokens it uses, how many rounds it takes, and how much it needs to redo before finishing. That's what cost per successful task captures.

Common questions

Before you choose

Do I need to know how to code?

No. You don't need anything to read the comparison. To test, use option 1 with the apps alone. Option 2 requires Claude Code or Codex, and outside a subscription, an account with a credit card at each company.

I pay a monthly subscription, not as I go. Is this useful?

Per-token prices apply if you pay as you go directly to the company (through its API). For subscribers, they're a guide to which model to use for each type of task. With a subscription you don't pay per token, but the model that makes fewer mistakes also uses less of your quota.

So which AI is best?

For quality alone, Claude Fable 5.2 leads the leaderboard. For cost per task, Argon is cheapest among models with a leaderboard score, until its price doubles on Dec 1. For your task, the answer comes from your test.

I already subscribe to ChatGPT. Is Claude or Gemini worth subscribing to?

It depends on your task. Try option 1 with the free versions: the one that gets your task right most often is the one worth subscribing to.

How much does it cost?

The project and this guide are free. Testing the models costs whatever the companies charge, and the prompt tells the AI to show you the price first.
